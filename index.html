Frimley Fuel AllotmentsA Timeline of Events

Share this page: Facebook X LinkedIn WhatsApp
This timeline sets out, in plain English and date order, what is publicly known about how the Frimley Fuel Allotments came to be selected as the preferred site for a new hospital. Every entry is sourced from publicly available records: Freedom of Information responses, Companies House, Land Registry, the Charity Commission, council minutes, parliamentary records, and Frimley Health's own published documents. No confidential information has been used.
Background — The Land
1800 — The charity is established
The Frimley Fuel Allotments charity is established under the Frimley Act 1800 — a Parliamentary statute directing commissioners to allot land to produce a reasonable supply of fuel for the poor inhabitants of the hamlet of Frimley. The general framework for land inclosure was provided by the Inclosure Act 1801. The charity's purpose is to provide fuel and financial support to the poor residents of the local parish. The land — 265 acres of heathland and woodland near Old Bisley Road — is formally set aside for this charitable purpose. Registered with the Charity Commission as charity number 1161717.
Source: Frimley Act 1800 (primary legislation); Inclosure Act 1801; Charity Commission register 1161717
1988 — Pine Ridge Golf Club lease
Pine Ridge Golf Club is granted a 125-year lease on part of the FFA land, running until 2113 — 87 years still remaining as of 2026. The lease generates a minimum of £90,000 per year for the charity. The charity is financially self-sufficient and not under financial pressure to sell.
Source: Land Registry Title SY793956; FFA Annual Accounts 2024
Ongoing — Protected land
The FFA sits within a Site of Nature Conservation Importance (SNCI) and immediately adjacent to the Thames Basin Heaths Special Protection Area — protected under UK law. The entire site is covered by a Woodland Tree Preservation Order. Protected species include adders, slow-worms, common lizards, brown long-eared bats, stag beetles, grayling butterfly, silver-studded blue butterfly, and reed bunting.
Source: Surrey County Council designation records; ecological survey records

At a members' meeting on 12 October 2022, the Frimley Fuel Allotments (FFA) charity adopts revised constitutional objects. The constitutional amendment takes place in the same year as both the undisclosed developer introduction (February 2022) and the first non-disclosure agreement (NDA) (13 July 2022). The proximity of those three events — developer introduced, NDA signed, power to sell formally incorporated — raises questions about whether the constitutional amendment was taken in anticipation of the transaction rather than independently of it.

Trust tells trustees existing hospital will be sold for housing
A meeting takes place between Frimley Health and the FFA trustees. Frimley Health tells the trustees: (1) that the existing Frimley Park Hospital site will likely be sold for housing once the new hospital is built; (2) that any public consultation will be "couched in general terms and not be site specific." HWRA believes this meeting marks the point at which the deal was effectively agreed.

The NHS Constitution for England — which all NHS bodies are required by law to take account of — gives patients and the public a legal right to be involved "in the planning of healthcare services commissioned by NHS bodies, the development and consideration of proposals for changes in the way those services are provided, and in decisions to be made affecting the operation of those services." This right attaches at the planning and proposal stage, not after a decision is made. Frimley Health's stated intention in June 2023 to keep consultation "couched in general terms and not be site specific" was a predetermined plan to structure public involvement in a way that could not meaningfully engage the decision being made. The constitutional right to involvement in "the development and consideration of proposals" is not satisfied by engagement deliberately kept too general to allow those proposals to be scrutinised or challenged.

NHS England's guidance on Planning, Assuring and Delivering Service Change for Patients also requires that the public be involved in options appraisal from the outset — including in "criteria setting and options appraisal" — before any preferred option is selected. The legal test for a fair consultation, the Gunning Principles (which the guidance explicitly requires to be met), states that proposals must be "still at a formative stage" when the public is consulted. The Trust's stated plan in June 2023 to keep engagement deliberately non-site-specific meant that no genuine public options appraisal could ever take place. The stage was already set.
Source: HWRA meeting minutes, 3 July 2026

31 January 2024
Duty to notify Secretary of State of substantial NHS reconfigurations enters into force
The National Health Service (Notifiable Reconfigurations and Transitional Provision) Regulations 2024 come into force. Under these regulations, NHS commissioning bodies are legally required to notify the Secretary of State of any "notifiable reconfiguration" — defined as a reconfiguration that would trigger the duty to consult a local authority under the Local Authority (Public Health, Health and Wellbeing Boards and Health Scrutiny) Regulations 2013. NHS England's own guidance states that "a change of site from which services are delivered, with its consequent impact on patient and visitor travel times, could be a substantial change, even with no changes to the type of services provided." The proposed relocation of Frimley Park Hospital to the Frimley Fuel Allotment (FFA) site — a change of hospital location with material travel time consequences for patients, visitors and emergency services — falls squarely within that definition. A notification to the Department of Health and Social Care (DHSC) would have engaged the Secretary of State's statutory power to call in and direct the reconfiguration. From this date, that notification was legally required if the proposal constituted a substantial variation. No such notification has been made public.
Source: National Health Service (Notifiable Reconfigurations and Transitional Provision) Regulations 2024 (SI 2024/15); NHS England, Planning, Assuring and Delivering Service Change for Patients (updated June 2026)

Trust argues existing site is not viable
Frimley Health presents its case that rebuilding on the current site is not viable. Three reasons: significant disruption; greater expense than an alternative site; and inability to complete by 2030. No planning constraints, flood-risk, or ecological concerns about the existing site are cited. The 2030 deadline has since been extended to 2032/33.

HM Treasury's Green Book — the mandatory framework for appraising all significant public spending proposals — requires that a shortlist of options must include a "business as usual" and a "do minimum" option, and that these must always be taken through to formal appraisal regardless of whether they appear to meet the objectives. The Green Book states explicitly that the purpose of this requirement is to ensure "decision makers are presented with genuine choices rather than a single pre-determined outcome." There is no published appraisal showing that rebuilding on the existing site was assessed against these requirements before being ruled out.

22 June 2024
JHOSC Chair publishes site criteria during election purdah — preferred site must be "available for sale June/July 2024"
Twelve days before the 4 July 2024 general election — during the pre-election purdah period, when public bodies including National Health Service (NHS) trusts are restricted from making public communications — the Chair of the Joint Health and Overview Scrutiny Committee (JHOSC), Cllr Trefor Hogg, publishes a presentation on his personal Facebook account titled "Replacing Frimley Park Hospital — The Real Facts." The post dismisses public concern about the programme as "somewhat irrational" and shares what appear to be Frimley Health briefing slides.

The slides reveal the formal hurdle criteria that shortlisted sites were required to meet. Among them: the site must be "available for sale June/July 2024" — meaning, as of this post, the programme expected a preferred site to be under contract within days. Other criteria include being "ideally brownfield," within five miles of the existing hospital, not encroaching on the Thames Basin Heaths Special Protection Area (SPA), and being "easily accessible for staff, patients and good emergency response times."

Two sites are assessed and marked FAIL: the London Road block (too small at 5.36 acres; major road and services issues) and all of Camberley Town Centre (no chance of availability for sale; not capable of development). No assessment of the Frimley Fuel Allotments (FFA) is published. FFA — announced as the preferred site two years later — is wooded allotment land held by a registered charity, not brownfield. The first JHOSC meeting had taken place just five weeks earlier, on 17 May 2024.

6 September 2024
Transport assessment completed and given to County Highways — junctions already at capacity
At the 6 September 2024 JHOSC meeting, Cain Thomas (Interim Programme Director, New Frimley Park Hospital Programme) tells the committee that a transport assessment has been completed and issued to the County Highways Authority (CHA). He states verbatim: "There were some junctions and areas in the local vicinity of the preferred sites that was at capacity and would need significant infrastructure upgrades."

This is a completed, submitted transport assessment — not a scoping exercise or a promise of future work. It finds the existing road network at the preferred sites to be inadequate, requiring significant upgrades before a hospital could function there.

Cllr Trefor Hogg chairs the meeting and raises no challenge. He does not ask which junctions are at capacity, what the estimated cost of the infrastructure upgrades is, whether any sites have been discounted on highway grounds, or whether the committee could see the assessment itself.

Ten months later, Hogg signs a letter to the ICB concluding there will be "no negative impact on access." That conclusion is drawn in the knowledge — from his own committee's proceedings — that a transport assessment already existed showing junctions at capacity.

The decision to proceed with known capacity constraints and no remediation plan engages Article 2 of the European Convention on Human Rights (right to life), incorporated into domestic law by the Human Rights Act 1998. Chronic road congestion around the hospital site risks degrading emergency vehicle response times — a life-safety consequence that was available to decision-makers at this point and was not addressed. The transport assessment also constitutes environmental information for the purposes of the Environmental Information Regulations 2004 (EIR 2004), meaning it should be disclosable on request within 20 working days. It has not been published.

Also documented at this meeting is the JHOSC Chairman's written answer to a question from Cllr Crawford (Hampshire County Council) asking whether the committee should be consulting MPs. Hogg's written reply states: "As Chair of the JHOSC I have already met with Dr Pinkerton the MP for Surrey Heath, and we have discussed our common view that it is imperative the Frimley Park replacement project is successful." This is only the third meeting of the committee, and the site has not yet been selected. The scrutiny chairman has placed on the public record that he regards it as imperative the programme he is scrutinising succeeds — a pre-committed advocacy position, not the neutral starting point of an independent scrutineer. It directly contradicts any later claim of impartiality.
Source: Surrey County Council MyCouncil — Agenda Item 15/24, JHOSC (Frimley Park Hospital), 6 September 2024, section "Engaging County Highways Authority" (URL: https://mycouncil.surreycc.gov.uk/mgAi.aspx?ID=40784); JHOSC 6 September 2024, Member Questions Supplement — written answer by Cllr Trefor Hogg to Q2 (Cllr Crawford, HCC)

4 July 2025
JHOSC Chair and Vice-Chair write to NHS endorsing no formal public consultation
Cllr Trefor Hogg (Chair) and Cllr Bill Withers (Vice-Chair) of the JHOSC write a letter formally concluding that no public consultation is required — on the grounds that there is "no loss of capacity planned, no reduction in the range of services planned." The letter is written before the preferred site has been publicly announced. Critically, it concludes there will be "no negative impact on access" — despite a transport assessment completed in Summer 2024 having already found that junctions near the preferred sites were "at capacity and would need significant infrastructure upgrades." That finding was disclosed to Hogg's own committee at the September 2024 JHOSC meeting, which he chaired. The detailed traffic impact study for the surrounding road network — including school routes — had not yet begun. It is submitted to the ICB Board as supporting evidence for the decision to bypass statutory consultation. Residents have no knowledge of this letter, or of the no-consultation decision being made, for nearly a year.

The NHS (Overview and Scrutiny) Regulations 2013 require NHS bodies to consult Overview and Scrutiny Committees on substantial variations in health services and to secure independent scrutiny of those proposals. A question remains whether this letter — written before the preferred site was announced and without independent assessment of alternatives — satisfied that legal requirement, or whether a £1.8–2 billion hospital relocation constitutes precisely the kind of substantial variation the Regulations were designed to capture.

Dr Al Pinkerton's letter of 12 August 2026 discloses that several JHOSC committee members have told him they were unaware of this letter — suggesting it did not represent a formal committee determination, but the personal position of the Chair and Vice-Chair acting on behalf of a body whose members had not been informed.
Source: JHOSC Chair/Vice-Chair letter, 4 July 2025 (Appendix 1, ICB Board Paper — released via FOI by Dr Al Pinkerton MP, published alpinkerton.uk)
NHS Frimley ICB Board formally resolves: no public consultation required
The NHS Frimley Integrated Care Board holds a public board meeting and formally resolves that the New Frimley Park Hospital programme "does not represent a substantial variation in service delivery requiring formal public consultation." The Board cites the Hogg/Withers letter as supporting evidence. The local MP is noted as having been briefed. The decision is now locked in — 11 months before residents are told which site has been chosen.

The Public Sector Equality Duty under Section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 requires NHS bodies to have "due regard" to equality and human rights impacts before making decisions. No Equality Impact Assessment covering this decision has been published. The duty was engaged at this point — and was not met.

The no-consultation determination also engages a second legal obligation: the NHS Constitution for England confers on patients and the public a legal right to be involved "in the development and consideration of proposals for changes in the way those services are provided." This right attaches at the planning and proposal stage — not after a decision is made. The no-consultation determination of 22 July 2025 denied this right at precisely the moment it was engaged. The June 2023 meeting record — in which Frimley Health told the Frimley Fuel Allotments (FFA) trustees that public consultation would deliberately be kept "couched in general terms and not be site specific" — establishes that the intent to circumvent meaningful involvement was formed two years before the formal Integrated Care Board (ICB) Board determination was made.

A third body of national guidance was also bypassed. NHS England's Planning, Assuring and Delivering Service Change for Patients sets out a mandatory staged process for substantial service changes: a case for change, a robust options appraisal with public involvement, a Pre-Consultation Business Case (PCBC), formal assurance by NHS England (the Stage 2 assurance checkpoint), and only then public consultation. There is no public evidence that FHFT or the ICB produced a PCBC, submitted it to NHS England for Stage 2 assurance, or followed the mandatory staged process at any point. The programme moved directly from internal planning to a no-consultation determination — bypassing the assurance stages designed to protect the public interest. Separately, the National Health Service (Notifiable Reconfigurations and Transitional Provision) Regulations 2024 — in force since 31 January 2024 — required the ICB to notify the Secretary of State of any reconfiguration constituting a substantial variation in health services. NHS England's own guidance states that "a change of site from which services are delivered, with its consequent impact on patient and visitor travel times, could be a substantial change, even with no changes to the type of services provided." On that definition, a notification to the Department of Health and Social Care (DHSC) was required. That notification would have engaged the Secretary of State's statutory call-in power. No such notification has been made public.
Source: NHS Frimley ICB Board Minutes, 22 July 2025 (released via FOI by Dr Al Pinkerton MP, published alpinkerton.uk)

12 December 2025
Government report confirms Frimley Park can operate safely beyond 2030 — and potentially to 2040
The Department of Health and Social Care publishes an independent strategic review by Mott MacDonald of the seven RAAC hospitals — including Frimley Park — collectively known as the "RAAC 7." The report confirms the hospitals "can remain open beyond 2030" with appropriate remediation and maintenance, and considers RAAC planks technically serviceable to "2030 plus 10 years" — approximately 2040 — with sustained management.
